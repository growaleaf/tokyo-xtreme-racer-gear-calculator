What this computes, and what it does not Top speed at redline in each gear, from the standard wheel-speed formula (see Sources below). It assumes no wheelspin and a perfectly rigid drivetrain — real cars (and this game) lose a little to tire growth and slip at high speed, so treat each number as a ceiling, not a guarantee.

Don't know circumference? Measure tire diameter in inches and multiply by 3.14159 — e.g. a 24in-diameter tire ≈ 75.4in circumference (the value preloaded above).

This is a 1950s-60s drag-strip approximation, not a game-accurate measurement It comes from a published power-to-weight curve fit across many real cars (see Sources) — it knows nothing about this game's actual drag, tire-grip, or AWD-transfer model, and cannot account for launch technique or traction. Treat it as a ballpark, not a prediction of what the game's own lap timer will show. No 0-60 or 0-100 figure is shown here because no similarly-attested public formula for those (from power-to-weight alone) could be verified — see README for what was checked and dropped.

Sources & last verified date

All claims below fetched directly 2026-10-20. This page gives no gearing verdict or setup recommendation anywhere — only the computed numbers.

  1. Wheel-speed formula (speed = RPM × circumference × 60 ÷ (gear ratio × final drive × 63,360)) — public-domain driveline physics, not a Tokyo Xtreme Racer constant, so no in-game gear-ratio numbers are hardcoded. Corroborated by two independently-fetched sources stating the equivalent formula: youcanic.com/vehicle-speed-rpm-calculator and firgelliauto.com's gear-ratio speed calculator, both giving speed(mph) = engine RPM × π × tire diameter(in) ÷ (gear ratio × final drive × 1,056) — algebraically identical to the formula used here once tire diameter is expressed as circumference (π × diameter) and the 1,056/63,360 unit constants are reconciled (63,360 ÷ 60 = 1,056). A worked example from youcanic.com (3,000 RPM, 3.73 final drive, 1.0 top gear, 28in tire → ~67mph) was independently re-derived against this page's formula and matched (see test.js Scenario 1).
  2. Quarter-mile ET / trap-speed approximation (Huntington formula): ET = 6.290 × (weightlb / hp)1/3 seconds, trap speed = 224 × (hp / weightlb)1/3 mph. Multiple independent calculator/reference pages agree on both the formula and its attribution to drag-racing writer Roger Huntington, who fit it from roughly 40-50 road-and-strip test runs in the 1950s-60s: omnicalculator.com/physics/quarter-mile and stealth316.com's 1/4-mile ET & MPH calculators. Both sources note this is the oldest and least precise of several later refinements (Fox 1973, Hale 1980s) that agree with Huntington within roughly 5%; this page uses the original Huntington constants since they are the most consistently attributed across sources. This formula is explicitly an empirical curve fit across many different real cars, not a physics simulation, and is not a Tokyo-Xtreme-Racer-specific claim.
  3. 0-60 / 0-100 time — explicitly NOT shown: search for a comparably clean, consistently-attributed public formula deriving 0-60 or 0-100 time from power-to-weight ratio alone turned up only uncredited forum rules-of-thumb with inconsistent units and no stated derivation — not a citable source by this lane's standard. Per this lane's failure protocol, that section was dropped rather than shipped with a fabricated or uncited formula; the speed-per-gear chart and the quarter-mile estimate above do not depend on it.
  4. Per-car reference setups — explicitly NOT shown: one build-guide site (treyexgaming.com) publishes a specific EK9 Civic Type R "Spoon Close Ratio Gear Set" (1st 2.533, 2nd 1.944, 3rd 1.591, 4th 1.280, 5th 1.033, final 4.400), but no second, independent source could be found stating the same specific numbers — Steam Community discussion threads for this game give only general qualitative advice ("lower final drive trades top speed for acceleration," "~4.1 final is a reasonable balance") without matching per-car figures. Per this lane's 2-source rule for any hardcoded per-car figure, no preset is shown; every number in the calculator above is typed in by the player.
